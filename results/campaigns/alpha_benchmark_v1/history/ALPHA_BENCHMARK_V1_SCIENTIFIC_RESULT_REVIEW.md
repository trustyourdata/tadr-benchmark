## Executive scientific summary

The campaign demonstrates repeatable execution and substantial detection of the deliberately constructed conditions within Check Set A. It does not demonstrate general accuracy, calibration, downstream model quality, regulatory suitability, production safety or state-of-the-art performance. All rates below describe a purposive synthetic corpus; paired formats and repeated executions are correlated observations.

The 320 primary correctness cells have 246 detections among 314 positive physical opportunities (78.3439%), with complete report coverage. All 252 INFO-inclusive expected final Finding opportunities were detected at their labeled severity. Those two facts are compatible: physical truth includes conditions below warning thresholds, support/applicability guards and known Alpha limitations. The candidate's whole-scenario normative conformance is 234/320 (73.1250%), not 100%.

The 86 nonconformant cells comprise 68 extra HIGH parse Findings on legitimate `event_key` identifiers and 18 category-cap annotation disagreements. The former remain an unfavorable practical specificity result, even though the pinned name heuristic explains them. The latter reflect incomplete benchmark labels, not evidence of faulty Core capping. Both require an explicit distinction between intended data semantics and the implemented Alpha contract; changing the benchmark's labels is outside this review.

Sampling has material consequences: 28/36 paired Finding sets agree exactly, and eight disagree in ways explained by the declared deterministic geometry. In the leakage/head case the bounded report loses the leakage Finding and hard gate and raises readiness from 60 to 100. Repeatability does not cure this limitation. All 72 selected scenario/context repeat pairs have identical original canonical bytes and hashes; no deliberate or unexpected context sensitivity was observed. The 42 format mismatches preserve Findings, scores, gates and remediation and are confined to representation-sensitive profile fields.

The evidence itself remains intact and reusable. Freeze is held for a localized benchmark-contract adjudication, not because some Core results are poor. The validity register below contains **1 CRITICAL, 2 IMPORTANT and 1 MINOR issue**; issue counts are distinct adjudication topics, not counts of affected rows.

## Adjudication of all 86 nonconformant scenario cells

Two disjoint primary classifications account for all 86 rows. Secondary explanations are not additional counts. All are standard/full_reference/repeat-0 correctness cells; neither sampling disagreements nor format comparisons are added to this denominator.

**B — 68 practical unexpected/false-positive Findings on a valid identifier.** Original sources contain non-null identifier strings `event_<integer>`, not intended dates or numeric corruption. The reports themselves infer `event_key` as text but contain `schema.parse_failures::column:event_key`, HIGH, confidence 0.62, with 10,000 canonical attempts and 10,000 failures. The frozen baseline's date-name heuristic includes the token `event` and can infer an expected datetime type from that name even on an ID column. That explains the coercion attempts; it does not make the identifier physically malformed. This is a real adverse specificity/heuristic limitation (secondary G), not a transport failure or missed split/duplicate detector.

The pinned-contract annotation also needs adjudication (secondary H): 66 of these cells explicitly declare `schema.parse_failures` absent, and the two multicategory cells declare it only for `signal_x`, omitting `event_key` from the subject frame. Those declarations conflict with the hash-verified implementation's name-based expected-type rule. The review retains both statements: the warning is unwanted for the independently intended identifier semantics, while the implementation is following its own heuristic. It must not be silently relabeled as a favorable result.

**H — 18 incomplete category-cap expectations.** Every affected variant declares `expected_category_caps={}`, but the report has a category cap of 40 for leakage (14 cells) or inference_mismatch (4 cells). The independent annotation function populates only the quality-accumulation-k3 cap and treats an empty mapping as exact absence. Static inspection of the bound implementation confirms a per-category cap of 40 whenever the uncapped sum exceeds 40. For example, a CRITICAL leakage Finding at confidence 0.97 has an uncapped weighted penalty of 43.456, and a pair of CRITICAL inference Findings at 0.99 totals 72.072. Both legitimately cap at 40. At leakage support 200, confidence 0.92 gives 41.216. The actual cap records and category risks corroborate this explanation. Gates, expected Findings and severities still pass. No label was edited and no revised conformance rate is adopted.
